# Pilot and customer playbook

How to run a pilot with a company, decide together whether to go ahead, and then look after them as a customer. The same steps are in the tool: Owner Console → open a company → **Pilot tracker**, where each step can be ticked off and the numbers are tracked for you. This page explains the thinking and gives you wording to copy.

Mailboxes: send proposals and anything about price from **sales@**; answer "how do I..." questions from **assessment@**; ask for testimonials and ideas from **feedback@**.

---

## 1. The journey

| Stage | What it means | You are done when |
|---|---|---|
| Prospect | Interested, nothing agreed | They agree to a pilot |
| Pilot planned | Goals, group and dates agreed | The pilot starts |
| Pilot running | People are taking the assessment and learning | The end date arrives |
| Pilot review | You read out the results and they decide | They sign, extend, or decline |
| Customer: onboarding | Signed. The first 30 days | The 30-day review is held |
| Customer: active | Live. Reviews at 60 and 90 days, then regularly | They renew or leave |
| Paused or lost | Not going ahead for now | Set a date to get back in touch |

---

## 2. Before the pilot (about a week)

**Agree three things with the sponsor (the person who signs off):**
1. **Goals.** What would make the pilot a success? Pick three or four numbers. The tool offers standard ones: 90% take the assessment, 60% start learning, 25% of the average learning plan completed. Others: people who finished, quiet people (lower is better), and average score gain since the baseline.
2. **The group.** 10 to 30 people is plenty. A real team with a real manager works better than volunteers from everywhere.
3. **Dates.** 4 to 6 weeks, with a start date and an end date.

**Set it up (about 10 minutes):**
1. Owner Console → **+ Create company**. Enter the name, the plan and the contact details. A flat price or a per-seat plan with enough seats for the group.
2. Set the **focus areas** (drag the skills they care about to the top).
3. Invite their admin with **Add admin**. When they join, tick "Invite their admin" (it ticks itself).
4. Open **Pilot tracker**. Set the stage to **Pilot planned**, enter the sponsor, group size, dates and goals, and **Save details and goals**.
5. Send the kickoff email (below). Move the stage to **Pilot running** on the start date.

---

## 3. While the pilot runs

| When | Do this | In the tool |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Invite everyone in the pilot group (the admin can do it from the Team dashboard, or you can) | "Invite everyone" ticks itself |
| Week 1 | Once most people have taken the assessment, press **Save a baseline today**. Hold a short check-in call: what is blocking anyone? | Tick "Check-in call after week 1" |
| Week 2 | Open the company's Team dashboard. The Report lists who is quiet. Send the reminder email | Tick "Remind anyone who has not started" |
| Week 3 | Mid-pilot check-in with the sponsor. Share the Report (Print or save as PDF). Ask two or three people for one sentence of feedback | Tick the two items |
| Last week | Book the results meeting. Move the stage to **Pilot review** | |

If a goal is "Not yet" halfway through, the **Next steps** list on the tracker says what is behind: invitations waiting, people who have not taken the assessment, people quiet for 30 days.

---

## 4. The review and the decision

1. Press **Print the pilot summary**. It has the headline numbers, each goal marked Met or Not yet, and before-and-after figures. It leaves out your private notes and the health check, so it is safe to hand over.
2. Results meeting (30 minutes): (a) what you agreed at the start, (b) the goals and how they went, (c) what changed for the group, with the before-and-after table, (d) what people said, (e) the proposal.
3. Send the proposal from sales@: seats, price, start date.
4. Write any concerns they raised in the **Timeline**. Record the decision, then move the stage to **Customer: onboarding** (signed), back to **Pilot running** (extended), or **Paused or lost**.

---

## 5. After the sale

**First 30 days (onboarding).** Set the seats or plan on the company record. Invite everyone covered by the contract. Walk the admin through the Team dashboard and the Report. Help them announce it to the team (email below). Set the next check-in date, and book the 30, 60 and 90-day reviews.

**Keeping a customer (active).** Hold the 60 and 90-day reviews with the Report open. Set the **renewal date** and a reminder two months before. Ask whether another team could use it, and ask (with permission) for a testimonial.

**The health check.** Once someone is a customer, the tracker shows **Healthy**, **Needs attention** or **At risk**, with the reasons:
- 20% or more of people quiet for 30 days is amber; 40% or more is red.
- Fewer than half the seats in use after 30 days, or fewer than 60% having taken the assessment after two weeks, is amber.
- No next check-in set is amber; more than two weeks late is red.
- No more people starting to learn over two weeks or more is amber.
- A renewal within 60 days with the average plan under 30% complete is amber.

## 6. Wording to copy

**Kickoff email (to the group, from their sponsor or you)**
> Subject: Starting our skills pilot on [date]
> Hi all, we are trying Product Advisory Learning for [4 weeks]. Please (1) take the free assessment (about 10 minutes) at https://assessment.productadvisory.co using the invitation link in your email, then (2) open your dashboard and start the first course it recommends. Your company sees your level and progress, never your individual answers unless you choose to share them. Questions: assessment@productadvisory.co.

**Reminder (about week 2)**
> Subject: Quick nudge: your skills pilot
> Hi [name], a short reminder to [take the assessment / open your dashboard]. Ten minutes is enough to start. If you did not get your invitation link, reply and we will send it again.

**Announcement for a new customer's admin to send**
> Subject: Product Advisory Learning is now available to everyone on [team]
> After a successful pilot, we are rolling out Product Advisory Learning to the whole team. Your invitation is in your inbox. Take the assessment first: it shapes the courses you are given. Our goal this quarter is that everyone has [started their plan] by [date].

**Mid-pilot update to the sponsor**
> Subject: Pilot update: week 3
> Where we are: [x] of [y] people have taken the assessment and [z] have started learning. Goals: [list with Met / Not yet]. What is helping: [..]. What we will do this week: [..]. The attached Report has the detail.

**Thank-you after the results meeting**
> Thank you for the time today. As discussed, the proposal is attached: [seats] seats from [date]. If it would help to talk it over with anyone else on your side, I am glad to join.

## 7. What the numbers mean

- **Invited people who have joined**: people who joined out of everyone invited (joined plus invitations still waiting).
- **Took the assessment / started learning / finished**: shares of the people who have joined.
- **Average plan complete**: across people who took the assessment, how much of their personal course plan is done.
- **Quiet**: no activity for 30 days (and not finished).
- **Score gain since the baseline**: today's average overall score minus the average on the baseline day. It rises when people retake the assessment, so it is a later-stage measure, not a four-week one.

Small groups: with fewer than five people, treat percentages as a first look. The tool says so in plain words.
